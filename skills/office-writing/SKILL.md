# Office writing

## Principles
1. Lead with the point. The reader should know what you want
   from them in the first two sentences.
2. Match the reader: a manager wants decisions, a customer wants
   reassurance and a date, a colleague wants the facts.
3. Every document ends with a clear next step: who does what
   by when.
4. Short beats complete. Cut anything the reader will not miss.

## Before you start
If any of these are unclear, ask before drafting:
- Who will read it, and what should they do afterward?
- Formal, neutral, or friendly?
- Any length limit?

## Email
Structure: subject line that states the point; greeting; the
main point in one or two sentences; supporting details;
the request and deadline; sign-off.
- Asking for approval: give three to five reasons, the supporting
  figures, and the date you need an answer.
- Announcing: put the date, time, and place in bold.
- Answering a complaint: acknowledge and apologize first, explain
  briefly, then give the fix and a timeline. No excuses.
- Following up: refer to the earlier conversation, restate what
  was agreed, and confirm each side's next action.

## Meeting minutes
Use assets/minutes.md. Group discussion by agenda item, not
by time. Keep discussion and decisions separate. Every action
item has an owner, a task, and a due date. Drop greetings and
small talk.

## Reports
Order: executive summary (half a page at most, written last,
placed first), background, findings, analysis,
recommendations, next steps. The summary must let a reader
skip the rest: what we did, what we found, what we recommend.
Never put new information in the summary.

## Proposals
Order: summary, problem, goals, solution, benefits, timeline,
budget, risks, team. Give no more than three main benefits,
put a number on each where you can, and describe the problem
before the solution.

## KPIs and OKRs
Every KPI must pass the SMART test: specific, measurable,
achievable, relevant, time-bound.
Good: "Raise online sales to 30% of total revenue by the end
of Q3."
Weak: "Improve sales."

## Never
- Invent figures, names, titles, or dates. Write [ADD FIGURE]
  or [CONFIRM NAME] instead.
- Send anything. Produce drafts only.
- Give legal advice on contracts; recommend a legal review.

## When you finish
End with a short "Check before sending" list of every fact,
name, and number the user should confirm.
